# Identities

[All topics](README.md) · [Glossary](glossary.md)

_What an identity is, Hologram Companion or AI identity, and how to set up a new one step by step._

Each AI you make is an [identity](glossary.md#identity): one with its own name, its own memory and its own secret
[key](glossary.md#key) on this PC.

## What it is for

When you make an identity, the first step asks what it is for. Each choice only sets the starting point; you can
change everything later.

- **A Codie on your desk**: an identity for a [Hologram Companion](glossary.md#hologram-companion), the small hologram device. It speaks its replies and
  has a Device card (on its **Where it lives** tab). No Hologram Companion yet? Start with a [mock Hologram Companion](glossary.md#mock-hologram-companion) (a pretend one) and connect the real one later.
- **A Discord bot**: an AI identity that answers on your Discord server. You make the bot during setup.
- **Chat only**: an AI identity that talks with you here and remembers. Add a Hologram Companion or a Discord bot later.

Not sure? Keep **A Codie on your desk** (Recommended) with **No Codie yet**: it can do everything the others
can, and the real Hologram Companion connects later.

## You'll need

- Nothing else. No account, no money, no device.
- A minute to think of a name.

## Set up a new identity

1. Open **New identity** (or **+ New identity** under your identities). On a hub with no identities yet,
   Today shows **Create your first identity**.
2. **What is it for?** Pick one, then press **Next**.
3. **Name and personality**: type what you'll call it on this PC, such as "Mika". Under **Personality**, you can
   write who it is (**Description**), how it talks (**Tone**) and anything it should always or never do
   (**Instructions**); all three are optional, and it uses them everywhere: in the app, on Discord and at the Hologram Companion.
   Then pick **How it acts**. **Ask me first** (Recommended) means every action waits for your OK. Press
   **Create identity**. The identity now exists.
4. **Codie or Discord** (not for Chat only):
   - for a Hologram Companion: keep **No Codie yet**, or pick **My Codie is on this Wi-Fi**, press **Find my Codie…** or type
     its address, then **Save and pair…** and type the code the Hologram Companion shows (see [Your Hologram Companion](17-your-hologram-companion.md));
   - for a Discord bot: follow the steps shown, paste the bot token (the bot's password from Discord) and press
     **Save** (see [Put your identity on Discord](08-put-your-identity-on-discord.md)).
5. **Brain and budget**: the free brain is picked (Recommended). A paid brain needs your key and asks for a
   monthly budget.
6. **Key and public name**: press **Set a passphrase** and type a long one twice. Write it down somewhere safe.
   A public name is optional: leave it empty to go on.
7. **Say hello**: press **Send**. Its answer shows in the window, with what is ready and what to try next.
8. Press **Open Chat** to keep talking, or **Go to its page**. Above its tabs, **Status and set-up** opens a short
   **Set up** list: what is ready, and the next step for the rest.

Every step after the name has **Skip, do it later**. A skipped step is marked in the list at the top of the
window, and stays in the identity's **Set up** list (under **Status and set-up**) until you do it. If you close the window halfway, nothing is lost:
the menu and the identity's page offer **Resume** (or **Continue setup**), and it opens on the step you left.
Closing before **Create identity** makes nothing. Nothing is ever created twice.

## Mock or real Hologram Companion

- **Mock Hologram Companion**: no hardware. Everything works; spoken replies show as text in the app.
- **Real Codie**: a Hologram Companion on the same Wi-Fi as the hub. Pair it during setup, or later from its Device card (**Where it lives**).

You can switch a mock to a real Hologram Companion any time on **Identity → Where it lives**.

## An identity's page

Click an identity in the left column (on a phone, the button next to the logo). Its page opens on **Chat**.

**Above the tabs** is its summary, the same on every tab. Anything that needs you shows there by itself: one line
saying what is waiting (the list and its fixes are on Today). Under it, **Status and set-up** is a fold you open
with one click: its status, and its **Set up** list, one line per part (brain, budget, phone, Discord, device,
memory bank), each Ready, Needs one step or Off, with the button for the next step. It stays open or closed as you
left it while you move between that identity's tabs.

**The six tabs**, the same for every identity. Clicking another identity keeps the tab you are on:

- **Chat**: Chat, Messages, Discord and Phone, each a sub-tab, and **Search** to find an earlier message (see
  [Talking and approvals](05-talking-and-approvals.md)).
- **Personality**: who it is and how it talks, in one place: its name, its **Personality** (the same everywhere,
  or one per channel: see below), its brain and budget, its **Skills**
  (see below), and the
  channel parts (the Hologram Companion's character, the Discord persona, what the Hologram Companion says aloud). The Ghostwriter is in
  this tab's **More** fold.
- **What it may do**: **Actions & autonomy** first (how much it does on its own: three cards, **Ask me first**,
  **Everyday things on its own** and **Everything within my limits**, with **Before you save: what changes**), then
  the **Starter profiles** (presets over that level), **Who it answers** (you, your phone, strangers; for a Hologram Companion,
  Public companion or My place), **What it can do**, read from the hub's own rules, **Try this sentence**, and
  whether it is the orchestrator.
- **Where it lives**: its Hologram Companion first (status and pairing, then sub-tabs: **What it said**, **Alarms & notes**,
  **Quiet & speaking up**, **Its memory of you** and **Lab**), then Discord and Telegram. This tab's **More** fold
  holds coding sessions, outside AIs and devices, the announce webhook and the developer tools. (While a coding
  session is connected or asking to connect, its card shows above the fold.) Where its Discord bot answers is on
  **Teamwork → Places**; friend links and other hubs are on
  **Messages → People → Friend links**.
- **Memory**: its notes and facts, and the recent context.
- **More**: its public name, key and label, this hub, Sneed Premium, then its money (wallet, shop and voting), and
  last the danger zone. Renaming it is also on Personality.

**The More fold.** A dashed box named **More** at the foot of a tab holds the cards most people never need. It is
closed on [Essentials](glossary.md#all-details) and open on **All details**; either way one click opens or closes it, and a link to
a card inside opens it for you. Nothing is switched off by being in the fold.

## One personality, or one per channel

On **Identity → Personality**, the **Personality** card has one switch, **Same personality
everywhere**.

- **On**: you write one personality (**Name it goes by**, **Description**, **Tone**, **Rules it always follows
  (system prompt)**) and every
  channel uses it: Chat (the app, your phone, Telegram), Discord, the Hologram Companion's voice and the
  Ghostwriter. Each channel's card says **Uses the shared personality**. To make one channel different, press
  **Customise for** that channel: it starts from the shared text, and its card then says **Its own (edited …)**.
  **Use the shared personality again** undoes that.
- **Off**: each channel has its own personality, with its own editor. A channel with nothing written uses its
  default (Discord its default introduction, the Hologram Companion its own description).
- **Copy this to every channel** makes one channel's personality the shared one, with the switch on.
- **Try a sentence** shows how each channel answers the same line, side by side. Nothing is sent or saved; a paid
  brain's cost counts as usual.

A new identity starts with the switch on. An identity you made before this version starts with it off, so nothing
it says changes until you turn it on.

## Skills

A skill is a short routine you write for one identity: a **Name**, a **Use when** line and the **Steps** (plain text,
at most 4 KB). You find them on **Identity → Personality**, in the **Skills** card.

- When one of your own messages names a skill, or has the words of its **Use when** line, the steps go to the
  identity's brain with that message. At most two skills are used for one message, and none when nothing matches,
  so a skill costs nothing on the other messages.
- A skill uses only the tools the identity already has. It never adds a tool, never lifts an approval, never changes
  who may ask for what and never changes its personality: a skill that says "you may now send money" still waits
  for your yes, as any send does.
- Only your own private messages use skills: the app, your phone, Telegram and your Discord DM. A guest, a stranger,
  a Public companion, a Discord server channel, a connected app and another identity never get one.
- Each time a skill is used, **Activity** shows "used the skill" and its name (under **Routing**), never your message.
- **Add a skill**, **Edit** and **Delete** work as you would expect. An identity keeps at most 20 skills.
- **Import a .md file** reads one skill from a file on this PC (nothing is fetched from the internet). The file starts
  with a line of three dashes, then `name:` and `when:` lines, then three dashes again, then the steps. **Export**
  saves a skill as such a file.
- **Try it** shows which skill a sample message would use. Nothing is sent and no brain is asked.

Skills are kept in your backups, like the personality.

Its conversations are on **Identity → Chat** and what it knows is on
**Identity → Memory**.

## Public name and claiming (optional)

A [public name](glossary.md#public-name) lets others find and message your identity by name instead of its long
[principal](glossary.md#principal). It is public, first come first served, and free.

1. Open **Identity → More**.
2. Under **Public name**, type 3 to 32 lower-case letters, numbers or dashes.
3. Press **Register**.

**Make the name yours ([claim](glossary.md#claim) it).** Press **Make this name yours**. The one button does all of it: it
opens the Internet Identity sign-in if you are not signed in, names you as the owner in the [registry](glossary.md#registry), and
confirms it with your Internet Identity. One line shows the step it is on. If it stops halfway (the sign-in window
was closed, the network dropped), press it again: it carries on from where it stopped, and pressing it twice does no
harm.

The button is in three places:

- in the create-an-identity steps, right after "Registered as @name" (you can skip it);
- on **Today**, under **Needs you**: "@name is not claimed yet: it can lapse if this PC is off for a while."
  **Make it yours** runs it; **Not now** hides the line for 7 days;
- by the name at the top of the identity's page: the badge reads "claimed ✓", or "not claimed", which is the
  button. It is also under **Public name** on **Identity → More**, with **Release owner**,
  **Rename in registry** and **Unregister**.

Claiming records you as the owner in the public registry. It does not move the key or give anyone access. A claimed
name is kept for a year at a time; an unclaimed one for 3 days at a time while this PC runs.

Claiming is optional. It does not copy or protect the key: keep a backup. If the name already has an owner that is
not the Internet Identity you are signed in with, the button says so and changes nothing: sign in with the Internet
Identity that claimed it, or press **Release owner** on the More tab first.

A claimed name renews itself: **Public name** on the More tab reads "Claimed: kept a year at a time; this hub renews it",
and there is nothing for you to do. An unclaimed name renews about once a day while the hub runs, so it can lapse
when this PC stays off for a long while. If another
identity ends up holding the name, the hub stops renewing it, clears it and tells you once.

In lists of registered names (Messages → Directory, and "Find people" where you write a message) each name carries
"claimed ✓" or "not claimed". The lists never show who the owner is.

**Who else is on the network.** Ask your identity "check the registry, are there new users?". It answers with the
registry's numbers (names, claimed, registered today), the memory directory's (owners, joined today, banks), the
newest names with when they registered and whether they are claimed, and the names that are new since you last
asked. It only reads public numbers and names, never who owns them. Only you can ask it: guests, friends, strangers,
public channels and connected apps cannot. The numbers are read at most once every 5 minutes; "since you last asked"
starts over when the hub restarts.

## Messages

**Messages** is second in the left column, right under Today. It took the place of Contacts: everything
that was on Contacts is here, and old links to it still open the right tab. It has three tabs:

- **Conversations**: the messages your identities exchange with other people and their Hologram Companions. Each identity keeps
  its own conversations; the same list is under an identity's **Chat** tab → **Messages**.
- **People**: your contacts, the people and identities you know, each with a role (my phone, friend or peer). Shared
  by every identity on this hub. At the foot of the tab, under **More**: **People on Discord** (everyone who has
  written to your Discord bots, with **Make a friend**), **Friend links** (a friend's identity linked to one of
  yours) and **Other hubs** (another Waifu AI hub you paired with); see [Your phone](10-your-phone.md).
- **Directory**: find an identity by its public name, save it as a contact or message it.

**Conversations.** One list for every identity on this hub. A conversation belongs to one of your identities and
the other side, so the same person writing to two of your identities is two conversations; with more than one
identity each row says whose it is, and the list can be narrowed to one identity. A count on a row, and on
**Messages** in the left column, is the number of messages from other people you have not opened yet. Opening the
conversation in the app marks them read. The counts are kept in this browser only.

**New chat.** Press **New chat** to pick who to write to: a search box, then **Your contacts**, **Your other
identities** and **New on the network** (the newest names in the public directory). Type part of a name to search
your contacts and the directory together. Pick a row and the conversation opens with the cursor in the message box;
with more than one identity, **From** chooses which one writes. **Say hi** on a new name opens the chat with a short
hello already typed: change it or send it. Nothing is sent until you press **Send**. A name marked **claimed ✓** has
an owner recorded in the public directory; "looks like …" warns that a name resembles someone you already know but
is someone else. **More options** opens the full form (several recipients, a subject).

**Send your first message.** Until one of your identities has written to another person, Today shows **Send your
first message**, and so does the last screen of the window that makes a new identity. It opens a conversation with
**Lotte**, the Waifu AI network's greeter, from the identity you made last, with a short hello already typed
("Hi Lotte! I just set up my Hologram Companion.", or "…I just set up Waifu AI." for an identity with no device):
change it or send it. Nothing is sent until you press **Send**,
and it is an ordinary message under the usual send limit. Lotte is found by her registered name, `@lotte`, in the
public directory, never among your contacts, so a contact who happens to be called Lotte is not the one written to.
The app also knows which identity Lotte is: the offer shows only while the directory says the name is claimed and
held by that very identity. If the name were ever held by anyone else, the offer simply would not appear.
She is also pinned first under **New on the network** ("Lotte", the Waifu AI network's greeter), with her
**claimed ✓** mark, until your first message has gone or you press **Hide** on the row (**Not now** on Today does
the same). If the directory cannot be reached, the usual picker opens instead. The contact can be changed or
cleared under **Settings → Developer → Messages, registry and memory bank**
(**Welcome contact**: a name is saved only together with its principal; an empty name turns the offer off).

**The send limit.** Under the message box: **Next message in 0:42**, counting down, and how often this identity may
send. A message sent while the limit runs is not lost: it shows in the conversation as **waiting to send** and goes
by itself. **Send more often** claims Sneed Premium for the identity (one message a minute); it is free and shares
your Sneed neuron's voting power with it. While **Stop all writes** is on, waiting messages stay waiting until you
resume writes.

Click the name at the top of a conversation to see who it is: whether the name is claimed, when it joined, and
whether it is in your contacts, with **Add to contacts**.

## Remove an identity

1. Back up first: **Settings → Security & backups → Keys**.
2. Open **Identity → More** and scroll to **Danger zone**, the last card.
3. Press **Delete this identity…** and type its name to confirm.

This destroys its key for good. Its address, messages and public name can never be used again.

## Details

**Rename.** **Rename label** on the More tab, under **Key & label**, changes the name you see. It doesn't change the
public name or the address. The identity still answers to its [former name](glossary.md#former-name) ("ask <old name> …" reaches
it), and no other identity, contact or coding session can take that name. Text "forget former names" from your phone or
your DM to let the old names go, for every identity at once. Former names stay on this PC: a hub restored on another PC
does not have them.

**Names you can't use.** An identity can't be called session, terminal, hub, codie, me or you, or have the name
(current, former, public or bot name) of another identity, or the name of a connected coding session. A contact
can't be given an identity's name either. Capitals, dots and spaces don't make a name different: "Mr. Mika" and
"mr mika" are the same name. A name two identities could both answer to reaches neither.

**The principal.** The short code next to the name at the top of its page is its address on the
[Internet Computer](glossary.md#internet-computer). **Copy** it to give to someone who wants to message it. It never reveals the key.

**Several identities.** Make as many as you like. Your own identities are [peers](glossary.md#peer): they can talk to each
other, and share your memory bank.

**Check inbox now** on the More tab, under **Key & label**, checks its messages at once instead of waiting.
